How to take a good photo

  1. Ruler at the same height as the measured surface. Flat things (paper, leaf): ruler right next to them. Thick things (box, book): lay the ruler on top of the object – the app ignores the ruler there. Each cm of height difference costs about 2–4 % of area.
  2. Phone parallel to the surface, straight above the middle of the object – use 📐 Level camera: it shows a spirit level and takes the photo by itself once the phone is flat (within 1°). On iPhone the level works in Safari (other browsers there get no access to the tilt sensor).
  3. Step back and zoom 2× rather than getting close: less perspective, the sides of a box stay out of view. Object and ruler should still fill most of the picture. The level camera starts at 2× where the phone allows it; in the phone's own camera app tap “2×”.
  4. Ruler flat and straight, at least 10 cm of it visible, mm marks sharp. On a thick object, let it lie completely on the object – not hanging over the edge. Do not hold a tape – weigh it down.
  5. Plain background with a different color than the object, soft even light, no flash, no glare on glossy wrapping.

Checking and fixing the result

  • Zoom with two fingers (or the ＋/－ buttons on the photo), drag to move around. ⤢ shows the whole photo again.
  • Outline －/＋ on the photo shrinks or grows the selected area by one step – e.g. to drop a thin shadow edge.
  • ✏️ Fix outline: paint with a finger to erase parts (a shadow on one side) or add missing parts. Choose the brush size; zoom in for precision.
  • ↶ undoes the last change – every edit can be undone.
  • The red handles should sit on two tick marks. In ✋ Move mode grab a handle (it snaps to the nearest tick) or the red line (the ticks are read again there), or type the length.
  • If the object is not found at all, use ⬚ Box (drag around it) or 👆 Tap (tap on it).

Chrome AI (Gemini Nano, desktop Chrome only) runs locally – your photo never leaves your device. Everything else works on any device without it.
